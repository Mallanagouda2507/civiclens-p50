## 1. Executive Summary
Deploying artificial intelligence in civic governance introduces unique ethical responsibilities. Automated triage and clustering algorithms directly influence how municipal budgets, repair crews, and public resources are allocated across urban neighborhoods.

This audit evaluates the potential algorithmic biases within CivicLens and details the concrete structural safeguards implemented to ensure spatial fairness, digital inclusion, and equitable governance.

---

## 2. Spatial Reporting Disparity & Digital Divide Audit

### 2.1 The Vulnerability
In municipal grievance systems, citizen engagement is heavily skewed by the **digital divide**:
- Affluent urban neighborhoods with high smartphone penetration, tech literacy, and broadband connectivity file significantly higher volumes of complaints per square kilometer.
- Economically disadvantaged wards, informal settlements, and elderly populations file far fewer digital tickets, despite often experiencing more severe infrastructure neglect.

### 2.2 Risk of Unmitigated AI Prioritization
If a machine learning system prioritizes repair work orders based on raw ticket frequency or density, municipal funding will naturally concentrate in already privileged wards, exacerbating municipal inequality.

### 2.3 CivicLens Structural Mitigations
1. **Locality-Normalized Density Metrics:** The analytics subsystem (`backend/controllers/analyticsController.js`) normalizes complaint volume against ward geographical boundaries rather than using raw aggregate counts.
2. **Hazard Severity Decoupling:** Urgent ticket priority (`HIGH`, `URGENT`) is determined by **hazard type** (e.g. exposed live electric wires, burst water mains, open sewage) rather than ticket volume. A single critical hazard in an informal settlement receives higher dispatch priority than 20 cosmetic pothole complaints in an affluent suburb.
3. **Equitable Ward Filtering:** Municipal staff triage boards (`StaffTriagePage.jsx`) enforce ward-level sorting, preventing officers from selectively cherry-picking high-volume zones.

## 3. Fairness in Duplicate Complaint Clustering

### 3.1 The Vulnerability
Automated duplicate clustering algorithms risk **citizen silencing**:
- If an algorithm aggressively merges complaints that occur on the same long street (e.g. two potholes 200 meters apart on 100ft Road), the second citizen's legitimate report is discarded, leaving a hazard unaddressed.
- If citizen reports are deleted upon duplicate detection, the public loses trust in the portal's transparency.

### 3.2 CivicLens Structural Mitigations
1. **Strict Spatial Gating ($\le 75\text{m}$):** Text similarity is *never* evaluated in isolation. Stage 1 Haversine distance must confirm close physical proximity ($\le 75\text{ meters}$) before any duplicate candidacy is considered.
2. **Non-Destructive Clustering:** Duplicate complaints are **never deleted**. Both the master ticket and child duplicate tickets persist in the database.
3. **Citizen Endorsement Escalation:** Each linked duplicate increments `duplicateCount` on the master ticket and records the citizen's unique timestamp, acting as a civic endorsement that elevates triage visibility without silencing individual voices.

## 4. Bias Audit Matrix

| AI Component | Potential Bias Risk | Severity | Mitigation Implemented | Verification Mechanism |
|---|---|:---:|---|---|
| **Department NLP Classifier** | Colloquial or non-standard civic phrasing routed to `GENERAL` or misclassified. | Medium | Added colloquial civic terminology (`crater`, `manhole`, `waterlogging`, `sparking`) to feature keyword dictionaries. | Verified across 6 department test cases in `tests/aiEngine.test.js`. |
| **Haversine Distance Filter** | Flat planar distortion at non-equatorial latitudes causing unequal clustering radii. | High | Adopted spherical geodesic Haversine formula with constant $R = 6,371,000\text{m}$. | Verified identical distances at polar and equatorial test vectors. |
| **Duplicate Clustering** | Suppressing complaints on long arterial roads. | Critical | Enforced $75\text{m}$ radius cutoff; reports $> 75\text{m}$ apart are strictly treated as independent complaints. | Verified by negative distance test in `tests/aiEngine.test.js`. |
