## 1. Executive Summary
The development of CivicLens was governed by the core tenets of the **Agile Manifesto**. This document maps the 4 fundamental Agile values and 12 supporting principles directly to concrete engineering practices, architectural choices, and verification mechanisms implemented within the project.

---

## 2. The 4 Agile Core Values Mapped to CivicLens

| Agile Core Value | Traditional Interpretation | Practical Implementation in CivicLens |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Individuals and Interactions over Processes and Tools** | Prioritizing team communication over rigid bureaucracy. | Daily developer-stakeholder alignment on municipal usability; AI tools (Copilot, Claude) acted as collaborative pair programmers rather than process gatekeepers. |
| **2. Working Software over Comprehensive Documentation** | Valuing functional software increments over paper designs. | Prioritized passing executable software: 70 automated tests, live React Leaflet map UI, and resilient dual-mode database over static design docs. |
| **3. Customer Collaboration over Contract Negotiation** | Co-creating software with end users. | Incorporated simulated citizen and municipal ward officer feedback loops to refine duplicate radius thresholds and GIS pin colors. |
| **4. Responding to Change over Following a Plan** | Adapting rapidly to empirical feedback. | Shifted duplicate detection from fragile deep transformer microservices to native Node.js TF-IDF + Haversine when latency and dependency issues emerged. |

## 3. The 12 Agile Principles Mapped to CivicLens Engineering

| Principle # | Agile Principle Statement | CivicLens Technical Manifestation | Verification Evidence |
|:---:|---|---|---|
| **P-01** | Highest priority is to satisfy the customer through early and continuous delivery of valuable software. | Shipped functional vertical slices in every sprint: Auth in Sprint 1, AI Algorithms in Sprint 2, REST APIs in Sprint 3, and React UI in Sprint 4. | Git commit history (`master` branch commits `6465fdb` to `7f9dc9b`). |
| **P-02** | Welcome changing requirements, even late in development. Agile processes harness change for competitive advantage. | Adapted the persistence tier to support in-memory fallback without altering the public API surface when offline testing was required. | Dual-mode implementation in `backend/services/dataStore.js`. |
| **P-03** | Deliver working software frequently, from a couple of weeks to a couple of months. | Four 2-week sprint cycles resulting in verifiable, test-backed deliverables at each milestone. | Sprint ceremonies and milestone tags in Git. |
| **P-04** | Business people and developers must work together daily throughout the project. | Simulated daily standups reconciling municipal operational requirements (ward boundaries, SLA thresholds) with developer implementation. | Standup logs recorded in Evidence 2.3. |
| **P-05** | Build projects around motivated individuals; give them the environment and support they need, and trust them. | Empirically empowered developers with AI scaffolding tools, coupled with peer code review and unit testing safeguards. | Prompt library and human review records. |
| **P-06** | The most efficient and effective method of conveying information is face-to-face conversation. | Collaborative architectural pairing during algorithmic design of geospatial and text similarity formulas. | Human refactoring notes in Evidence 1.5. |
| **P-07** | Working software is the primary measure of progress. | Progress measured by passing automated test suites (70 passed tests, 84.25% line coverage) rather than estimated percent complete. | Verified test results in `test-results/backend-coverage.txt`. |
| **P-08** | Agile processes promote sustainable development. Sponsors, developers, and users should maintain a constant pace. | Steady velocity of 9 Story Points per sprint over 4 sprints (36 total SP) preventing developer burnout. | Velocity charts and sprint metrics. |
| **P-09** | Continuous attention to technical excellence and good design enhances agility. | Rigorous modular service architecture: `geoSpatialService.js`, `textSimilarityService.js`, and `departmentClassifierService.js`. | High modular cohesion; zero cross-service circular dependencies. |
| **P-10** | Simplicity—the art of maximizing the amount of work not done—is essential. | Rejected complex distributed microservices and heavy vector databases; implemented great-circle Haversine math and TF-IDF in pure Node.js. | Zero external C++ or Python runtime dependencies. |
| **P-11** | The best architectures, requirements, and designs emerge from self-organizing teams. | Continuous refactoring of the complaint state machine to enforce resolution remarks upon closure based on testing observations. | Lifecycle validation tests in `backend/tests/complaints.test.js`. |
| **P-12** | At regular intervals, the team reflects on how to become more effective, then tunes and adjusts its behavior. | Conducted Sprint Retrospectives after every sprint, adjusting AI prompt constraints and test assertions accordingly. | Retrospective notes in Evidence 2.3. |
